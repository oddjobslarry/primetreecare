Know The Signs

What Qualifies as a Tree Emergency?

A tree emergency is any situation where a tree poses an immediate risk to people, structures, vehicles, or power lines — including a fallen trunk, a split or leaning tree after wind, or large hanging limbs over a driveway or walkway. If a tree is actively failing or has already fallen, treat it as urgent and call a professional immediately rather than waiting for a scheduled appointment.
✓

Tree fallen on a house

Structural entry points, roof punctures, and hidden interior damage require immediate stabilization and assessment.

✓

Tree fallen on a garage

Garages often carry less structural reinforcement than homes, raising collapse risk under tree weight.

✓

Tree on a vehicle

Weight distribution and glass hazards mean vehicles should never be approached before a crew arrives.

✓

Tree blocking road access

Emergency vehicle access and daily safety both depend on quick, controlled clearing.

✓

Large hanging limbs

A "widowmaker" limb can drop without warning and requires careful rigging to bring down safely.

✓

Split trunk after storm

A split trunk is a structural failure — the tree can come down fully with little additional force.

✓

Leaning tree after high winds

A new or worsening lean often signals root-plate failure underground, not just visible above-ground damage.

✓

Tree touching power lines

Always treated as energized and life-threatening until a utility company or trained crew confirms otherwise.

✓

Uprooted tree

Lifted root plates are unstable and can shift further, even after the initial fall.

✓

Structural tree failure

Cracking, bark separation, or visible internal decay exposed by storm stress calls for same-day evaluation.

Right Now

What To Do Immediately After Tree Storm Damage

Stay back, stay safe, then call. The steps below, in order, protect people first and property second — most storm-related tree injuries in Chicago happen when homeowners approach damage before it's been assessed.
1

Stay Away From The Tree

Keep a safe distance from the entire canopy and root zone. Storm-stressed trees can continue shifting or dropping limbs well after the wind dies down.

2

Watch For Power Lines

Assume any line in contact with a tree is energized. Do not touch the tree, nearby fences, or standing water near the area.

3

Secure The Area

Keep children, pets, and neighbors away. Use cones, tape, or a visible barrier if it's safe to place one from a distance.

4

Contact Emergency Services If Needed

Call 911 for downed power lines, gas leaks, trapped individuals, or any active life-safety threat.

5

Document Damage

Photograph the tree, the affected structure, and surrounding area from a safe distance for your insurance file.

6

Contact Prime Tree Care

Call (773) 302-8351 for 24/7 dispatch. We triage by hazard level and get a crew moving.

7

Notify Insurance

Report the damage to your homeowner's carrier and share our documentation and arborist report to support your claim.

Utility Safety

Emergency Tree Service Around Power Lines

Any tree in contact with an energized power line should be treated as an electrical hazard, not a tree-removal job, until the utility confirms the line is de-energized. This is the single most dangerous category of tree emergency, and it's where DIY attempts most often turn fatal.

Chicago's dense residential grid means mature trees frequently grow into or near overhead utility corridors, especially after high-wind events. Our crews are trained to recognize and work safely around energized power lines, and we coordinate directly with the appropriate utility provider — ComEd in most of Chicago and Cook County — before beginning any cutting or rigging near a compromised line.

This is part of standard utility vegetation management practice: line clearance work near active infrastructure follows strict protocols, restricted access zones, and coordinated shutoffs where needed. We also perform a full tree risk assessment on arrival to determine whether the hazard is isolated to the tree itself or involves compromised utility hardware, splintered poles, or transformer proximity.

Why DIY is dangerous: downed or entangled lines can be energized even if they appear inactive, ground current can travel through wet soil, bark, and standing water, and a tree under tension from a line can release suddenly when cut. If you see a tree touching a line, back away, keep others away, and call your utility provider first — then call Prime Tree Care for the tree work itself.

⚠️ If a tree is touching a power line

  • Stay at least the height of the tree away from it
  • Don't touch the tree, branches, or attached fencing
  • Keep kids and pets well clear of the area
  • Call your utility provider to report the hazard
  • Call Prime Tree Care once the line is confirmed safe

Why It Happens

Common Causes of Emergency Tree Failures in Chicago

Most Chicago tree emergencies trace back to a handful of repeat causes: severe wind events, weakened root systems, and disease or pest damage that went undetected until a storm exposed it.
1

Derechos

Fast-moving, widespread straight-line windstorms capable of downing mature trees across entire neighborhoods in minutes.

2

Straight-Line Winds

Sudden downbursts push horizontally rather than rotating, snapping limbs and toppling shallow-rooted species.

3

Thunderstorms

Combined wind, saturated soil, and lightning strikes weaken trees that then fail hours or days later.

4

Ice Storms

Ice loading adds significant weight to canopies, snapping limbs that seemed structurally sound.

5

Snow Loading

Heavy, wet snow accumulation on branches is a common cause of limb failure in late-season storms.

6

Root Failure

Chicago's compacted clay soil restricts healthy root spread, making trees more prone to tipping in saturated ground.

7

Emerald Ash Borer Damage

EAB-infested ash trees become structurally unsound well before visible dieback is obvious. Learn about EAB in Chicago →

8

Dutch Elm Disease

DED weakens internal wood structure, raising failure risk during wind events. Compare EAB vs. DED →

9

Structural Defects

Included bark, prior improper pruning cuts, and co-dominant stems are common hidden failure points.

Can a Leaning Tree Be Saved?

Sometimes. A minor lean caused by soft, saturated soil can often be corrected with straightening and staking, while a lean caused by root-plate failure or trunk splitting almost always means removal is the safer, more cost-effective path.
ConditionStabilization PossibleRemoval Recommended
Minor lean (soft/wet soil)Often — staking or guyingUsually not necessary
Storm-shifted rootsSometimes — depends on root-plate integrityCommon if roots are torn or exposed
Split trunkRarelyAlmost always
Structural defects (prior)Depends on defect severityCommon if compounded by storm stress

Claims Support

Insurance Claims After Tree Damage

Most homeowner insurance policies cover emergency tree removal when the tree damages a covered structure — but coverage, documentation requirements, and mitigation expectations vary by carrier and policy.

🏠 Homeowner Insurance

Coverage generally applies when a tree damages your home, garage, fence, or another insured structure — not typically when it falls in open yard space.

📋 Documentation Requirements

Adjusters expect timestamped photos, a description of the event, and a professional assessment of damage and removal scope.

🛡️ Emergency Mitigation

Most policies require reasonable steps to prevent further damage — tarping a roof or securing an area — which we can assist with on-site.

📸 Photo Evidence

We photograph the tree, point of impact, and surrounding damage before and after removal for your claim file.

📄 Arborist Reports

A written report describing tree condition, cause of failure, and scope of work performed, suitable for adjuster review.

🤝 Claim Support

We're available to answer adjuster questions directly and clarify scope, timeline, and cost breakdowns as needed.

Note on terminology: an insurance claim for a fallen branch typically follows the same documentation path as a full tree removal — photograph first, then call us for mitigation and removal.

Straight Answers

Emergency Tree Service — Frequently Asked Questions

Who should I call if a tree falls on my house?

Call Prime Tree Care's 24/7 emergency line immediately. If the tree has damaged your roof, windows, or utility connections, also contact your insurance carrier, and call 911 if there's an active safety threat like a gas leak or downed power line. Our crews assess structural risk on arrival and begin safe stabilization and removal.

Is a leaning tree considered an emergency?

A tree that has visibly shifted or developed a new lean after wind activity, with lifted soil or cracked roots at its base, should be treated as an emergency and assessed the same day, even if full removal isn't ultimately necessary.

What if a tree is touching power lines?

Treat it as energized and dangerous. Stay back at least the tree's height, don't touch the tree or anything connected to it, and call your utility provider before calling us for the tree work.

How quickly can emergency tree crews respond?

We dispatch throughout Chicago and the suburbs around the clock. Response time depends on storm volume and location, but most calls are triaged and a crew is en route within hours, with life-safety hazards prioritized first.

Does insurance cover emergency tree removal?

Most homeowner policies cover emergency removal when a tree damages a covered structure. A tree falling in open yard space without hitting anything is often not covered. We provide documentation to support your claim either way.

Can a fallen tree be removed the same day?

In most cases, yes — once our crew completes a hazard assessment, controlled removal and debris clearing typically happen the same visit, provided site access, weather, and utility clearance allow it.

What should I do during a storm?

Stay indoors, away from windows facing large trees, and don't go outside to inspect damage until conditions are safe. Call for emergency service once the storm has passed.

Can storm-damaged trees be saved?

Minor limb loss or a slight lean is often correctable with pruning, cabling, or bracing. A split trunk or major root failure is typically not salvageable and should be removed for safety.

How much does emergency tree removal cost?

Typically $150–$600 for a fallen limb, $800–$2,500 for a small to medium emergency removal, and $3,500–$10,000+ for a large, crane-assisted removal, depending on accessibility and utility involvement.

Is emergency service available 24/7?

Yes — Prime Tree Care provides around-the-clock emergency tree response, including nights, weekends, and holidays, throughout Chicago and the surrounding suburbs.
