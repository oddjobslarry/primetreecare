Our Philosophy

Why South Side Trees Deserve Preservation-First Care

Few parts of Chicago carry as much tree canopy history as the South Side. From the century-old oaks lining Beverly's ridge streets to the legacy elms still standing in pockets of Hyde Park and Morgan Park, this is a part of the city where the trees are often as old as the housing stock itself.

Historic tree canopy: Mature trees throughout South Side neighborhoods aren't just landscaping — they're part of what defines these blocks. Decades-old oaks and large maples shape neighborhood identity, add measurable property value, cut summer cooling costs with real shade coverage, and provide environmental benefits like stormwater interception that a newly planted tree can't replicate for 40 or 50 years.

Preservation before removal: Our approach on every South Side property is the same: diagnose first, treat where it's realistically possible, and remove only when a tree presents a genuine, unmanageable safety risk. A tree showing early canopy decline or a treatable pest issue is not the same situation as a structurally failed tree leaning over a garage — we make that distinction with a documented Tree Risk Assessment, not a sales pitch.

Why mature trees are worth saving: A mature shade tree removed today isn't replaced in any meaningful sense for decades. Replacement saplings take 25–40 years to provide comparable shade and canopy volume. When a tree can be safely preserved through structural pruning, cabling, root management, or targeted treatment, that decision protects both the immediate landscape and the long-term canopy continuity of the block.

Decision Framework

Tree Preservation vs. Tree Removal

ISA-certified arborists don't default to removal — every recommendation follows a structured evaluation of condition, risk, and realistic treatment options.

ConditionPreservation Possible?Recommended Action
Minor canopy declineYes, in most casesSoil correction, fertilization, monitoring
Emerald Ash Borer (early-moderate)Often, if caught earlySystemic insecticide treatment
Emerald Ash Borer (advanced)RarelyRemoval, with diverse species replanting
Structural defects (minor)YesStructural pruning, cabling, or bracing
Storm damage (limited)OftenCorrective pruning, structural support
Advanced decayCase-by-caseRisk assessment; removal if structural integrity is compromised
Root failureRarelyRemoval — root failure is a high-priority safety risk

Every recommendation is grounded in a documented Tree Risk Assessment weighing the likelihood of failure against the consequences — proximity to a home, driveway, alley, or power line — rather than a blanket policy in either direction.

Storm Readiness

South Side Storm Preparedness Guide

South Side trees face a genuine seasonal risk cycle — knowing what each season brings helps property owners plan ahead instead of reacting after damage occurs.

Spring
40°F–65°F · Severe Storm Season Begins

Thunderstorms and straight-line winds increase through April and May, often before newly leafed-out canopies have hardened off — a high-risk window for limb failure.

Summer
70°F–85°F · Drought & Wind Risk

Heat and dry stretches stress root systems while severe thunderstorms and occasional derecho-style straight-line wind events remain a real threat through August.

Fall
45°F–65°F · Ideal Pruning Prep

Cooling temperatures make this the right window to schedule dormant-season pruning appointments and pre-winter risk assessments before ice season arrives.

Winter
15°F–35°F · Ice & Snow Loading

Heavy wet snow and ice accumulation add significant weight to weakened limbs, making previously unnoticed structural defects fail suddenly.

Before Storm Season

Schedule a Tree Risk Assessment on mature trees, especially oaks and maples with large canopy spread. Install cabling or bracing on trees with known structural weaknesses. Remove dead or hanging limbs proactively.

During Severe Weather

Stay indoors and away from windows near large trees. Never attempt to clear a downed limb or tree touching a power line — treat it as energized and call ComEd and emergency services.

After Storm Damage

Photograph damage for insurance before any cleanup. Avoid walking under hanging "widowmaker" limbs. Call a certified arborist to evaluate whether the tree can be stabilized and preserved or requires removal.

Ownership & Permits

Parkway Trees vs. Private Trees

One of the most common points of confusion for South Side homeowners is who's actually responsible for a given tree. A parkway tree — growing in the strip between the sidewalk and the street — is owned and managed by the Chicago Bureau of Forestry, and removal or major work typically requires going through the city. A private tree, anywhere on your own lot, is entirely your responsibility to maintain, insure, and remove when necessary.

This distinction matters for permitting too: private tree removal generally doesn't require a city permit, while parkway tree work does. It also affects who to call first when a tree is damaged or hazardous — city-owned parkway trees go through the Bureau of Forestry's request system, which can involve longer wait times than a private arborist call.

Common Questions

South Side Tree Service FAQ

Can mature trees be saved instead of removed?

Often, yes. A certified arborist performs a Tree Risk Assessment to evaluate structural integrity, decay extent, and canopy health before recommending removal. Many South Side trees showing early decline, minor deadwood, or treatable pest activity can be preserved through structural pruning, cabling, soil correction, or targeted treatment. Removal is recommended only when a tree presents an unmanageable safety risk or has progressed past the point where treatment can realistically extend its safe lifespan.

How much does tree removal cost on the South Side?

Cost varies based on tree size, species, structural condition, proximity to structures or power lines, and access. A small ornamental tree costs far less than a large, mature oak requiring crane support or tight-access removal near a garage or alley. Prime Tree Care provides a free, written, on-site estimate so South Side property owners know the exact cost before any work begins.

What is a Tree Risk Assessment?

A Tree Risk Assessment is a structured, ISA-based evaluation of a tree's likelihood of failure and the potential consequences if it fails, considering trunk condition, root health, canopy structure, and target proximity such as homes, driveways, or power lines. It's the foundation of every preservation-versus-removal decision Prime Tree Care makes, replacing guesswork with a documented, professional evaluation.

Can Emerald Ash Borer trees be treated?

Yes, in many cases. Ash trees caught in the early-to-moderate stages of Emerald Ash Borer infestation can often be saved with systemic insecticide treatment, particularly high-value or structurally sound trees. Once canopy dieback exceeds roughly 30–50%, treatment success drops significantly and removal typically becomes the safer, more cost-effective option. Early detection is the deciding factor.

What causes canopy decline?

Canopy decline on South Side properties is commonly caused by root zone compaction, construction damage, drought stress, aging, pest pressure such as Emerald Ash Borer, disease, and girdling roots. Because canopy dieback is often a visible symptom of an underlying root or soil issue, a full site evaluation is usually needed to identify the actual cause rather than treating the canopy alone.

How often should mature trees be inspected?

Mature trees on South Side properties should generally be professionally inspected every 1–2 years, and immediately after any major storm event, construction activity nearby, or visible changes like leaning, cracking, or sudden leaf loss. Legacy oaks, elms, and large maples in historic neighborhoods benefit from more frequent monitoring given their age and structural investment.

Do I need a permit to remove a tree?

It depends on whether the tree is on private property or a city-owned parkway. Private trees generally don't require a City of Chicago permit to remove. Parkway trees, located in the strip between the sidewalk and street, are owned and managed by the Chicago Bureau of Forestry, and removal typically must go through the city rather than a private contractor alone.

Who is responsible for a parkway tree?

The City of Chicago's Bureau of Forestry owns and is responsible for parkway trees — those growing in the public strip between the sidewalk and the street. Homeowners are responsible for trees located entirely on their own private property, including maintenance, insurance, and removal.

What is the best time to prune trees in Chicago?

Late winter, while trees are dormant, is generally the best time for structural pruning in Chicago, as it reduces disease transmission risk and lets arborists see branch structure clearly without leaf cover. Oak pruning specifically should avoid spring and early summer months due to oak wilt risk, and elm pruning should avoid the Dutch Elm Disease transmission season for the same reason.

What should I do after storm damage?

Stay away from any tree with broken limbs still hanging (known as "widowmakers"), downed branches near power lines, or a visibly leaning trunk, and call a professional immediately rather than attempting cleanup yourself. Document the damage with photos for insurance, and request an arborist evaluation to determine whether the tree can be stabilized and preserved or needs removal.
