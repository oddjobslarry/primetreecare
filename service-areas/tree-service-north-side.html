Ownership & Responsibility

Parkway Trees vs. Private Trees on Chicago's North Side

This is the single most common point of confusion for North Side homeowners, and getting it wrong can mean an unnecessary permit request or an unauthorized removal of a tree the city owns.

What Is a Parkway Tree?

A parkway tree is planted in the strip of land between the sidewalk and the street — the "parkway." On tree-lined North Side blocks in Lincoln Park and Ravenswood, these are often the largest, most mature trees on the block.

Who Owns Parkway Trees?

The City of Chicago owns every parkway tree. This holds true whether the block is in Lakeview, Andersonville, Irving Park, or anywhere else in the city — the adjacent homeowner does not own the tree in front of their house.

Who Handles Maintenance?

The Chicago Bureau of Forestry handles routine maintenance, pruning cycles, and removal decisions for parkway trees. Residents can request service but cannot authorize private removal or major pruning themselves.

When Does the Bureau of Forestry Get Involved?

Any time a parkway tree needs pruning, has visible hazard signs, or requires removal, the request routes through the Bureau of Forestry — typically initiated via CHI311.

When Should Homeowners Call an Arborist?

For anything on private property — your own yard, side lot, or a tree overhanging from a shared property line — an arborist inspection is the right first step, and can also help document a parkway hazard to support a city request.

For example, a leaning parkway oak on a Ravenswood block or a storm-damaged elm along the curb in North Center goes through the Bureau of Forestry, while a backyard silver maple threatening a garage in Roscoe Village is entirely the homeowner's call. Specialized Expertise

Utility-Aware Tree Pruning in Dense Urban Neighborhoods

Overhead utility infrastructure runs through nearly every alley and many streets across the North Side, and pruning near it wrong causes problems that outlast the job by years.

Common Utility Conflicts

Power lines running along alleys behind Lakeview two-flats, communication lines strung between poles in Uptown, and service drops connecting individual homes throughout Edgewater all create points where tree limbs and infrastructure compete for the same space.

Safe Arborist Practices

Our crews use aerial lifts and bucket trucks to reach clearance zones safely, follow utility clearance requirements for each line type, and coordinate directly with the utility when a line needs to be flagged or de-energized before work begins.

Why Improper Pruning Creates Long-Term Problems

Topping or hacking a tree back from a line without proper technique triggers weak, densely clustered regrowth that grows back into the clearance zone faster than a properly pruned cut would. Over years, this pattern causes structural defects, accelerates tree decline, and increases future failure risk — exactly the outcome utility-aware pruning is designed to prevent.

Common Tree Species Found Across Chicago's North Side

Knowing the species on a block tells our arborists what to look for before we even reach the tree.

Oak Trees

Long-lived and structurally strong, but oak wilt is a real concern in Chicago — pruning is best scheduled outside the high-risk spring window, and structural pruning helps these trees age safely on tight urban lots.

Maple Trees

Fast-growing and common throughout North Center and Roscoe Village, but that same fast growth produces weaker wood, making mature maples more susceptible to storm-related limb failure.

Elm Trees

Chicago's remaining mature elms require ongoing Dutch Elm Disease monitoring, since the disease still moves through root grafts and bark beetle activity on established blocks.

Honey Locust

A common newer parkway planting thanks to strong urban tolerance for compacted soil and pollution, though structural maintenance in early years shapes a stronger, safer canopy long-term.

Ash Trees

Remaining ash trees across the North Side continue to face Emerald Ash Borer pressure — early detection is the difference between a treatable tree and a removal.

1

Dead Branches

Brittle, bark-stripped limbs with no live growth are a fall risk in any wind.

2

Leaning Trunks

A new or worsening lean, especially near a home or sidewalk, needs prompt evaluation.

3

Cracks

Vertical or horizontal trunk cracks signal internal stress close to failure.

4

Open cavities reduce the structural wood available to bear weight and wind load.

5

Lifted sidewalk slabs or soil near the base often point to root or anchoring issues.

6

Bark Loss

Missing or peeling bark exposes the tree to decay and pest entry points.

7

Canopy Thinning

A crown that's visibly sparser than neighboring trees can signal root or disease stress.

8

Utility Line Contact

Any branch touching a power or service line needs immediate professional attention.

9

Storm Damage

Broken limbs or a cracked leader after a wind event should be checked before the next storm.

10

Fungal Growth

Conks or mushrooms at the base are one of the clearest visible signs of internal decay.

Common Questions

North Side Tree Service FAQs

Who is responsible for a parkway tree in Chicago?

The City of Chicago's Bureau of Forestry owns and is responsible for parkway trees — the trees planted in the strip between the sidewalk and the street. On the North Side, this covers the mature street trees lining blocks throughout Lakeview, Lincoln Park, Ravenswood, and similar neighborhoods. Homeowners are not responsible for pruning, treating, or removing a parkway tree, though they can and should report hazards through CHI311.

Can I trim a parkway tree myself?

No. Because parkway trees are City of Chicago property, residents should not cut, prune, or remove them without going through the Bureau of Forestry. Unauthorized pruning can damage the tree, violate city ordinance, and create liability. Requests go through CHI311, and a licensed arborist can help document the issue to support that request.

Do I need a permit to remove a tree?

Private trees on your own North Side property generally don't require a city permit to remove. Parkway trees are different — they belong to the city and go through the Bureau of Forestry rather than a private removal permit. Trees near a property line or shared fence often benefit from arborist documentation before removal.

How much does tree removal cost on the North Side?

North Side removal costs follow the same size factors as the rest of Chicago — roughly $300–$800 for small trees, $1,500–$3,000 for medium trees, and $6,000–$9,000+ for large mature trees — but dense lot spacing, alley access, and proximity to garages and power lines can add to the total. See our full Tree Removal Cost in Chicago guide for a detailed breakdown.

What should I do if a branch touches a power line?

Stay away from the tree and the branch, keep others and pets clear, and don't attempt to cut or move it yourself. Contact ComEd to report the hazard and call a licensed, utility-aware arborist. Never assume a line is de-energized.

How often should mature trees be pruned?

Most mature North Side street and yard trees benefit from structural pruning every 3–5 years, with more frequent deadwood and hazard checks for older or storm-exposed trees. Utility clearance pruning follows a separate cycle set by the utility.

What tree species are most common on the North Side?

The canopy includes a mix of oak, maple, elm, honey locust, and remaining ash trees. Honey locust and select oak and elm cultivars are common newer parkway plantings, while older blocks still carry mature silver maple and green ash.

Can Emerald Ash Borer trees be saved?

Ash trees caught early can often be saved with systemic insecticide treatment on a recurring schedule. Once infestation causes significant canopy dieback — generally more than 30–50% crown loss — removal becomes the safer, more cost-effective option.

What is a Tree Risk Assessment?

A structured evaluation by a certified arborist examining a tree's structural condition, decay, lean, root stability, and proximity to targets like homes, sidewalks, or power lines to determine the appropriate next step.

When should I call an emergency arborist?

Call immediately if a tree or large limb has fallen on a structure, vehicle, or power line, if a trunk has split, if a limb is hanging over a sidewalk or parked car, or after any severe storm that leaves visible cracking or lean.
