Local Problems We Solve Every Week

The Tree Issues That Are Unique to Chicago

Generic tree-care advice doesn't account for what actually happens to trees in this climate and soil. Here's what we deal with on Chicago properties, all season long.

Emerald Ash Borer

A large share of Chicago's tree canopy is ash, and EAB has spread through nearly every neighborhood. We check for D-shaped exit holes, canopy dieback, and bark splitting to catch it while treatment is still an option.

Heavy Clay Soil

Compacted clay across much of Cook County restricts root growth and drainage. Our fertilization and planting plans are built around correcting this, not generic national soil guidance.

Severe Wind & Storm Events

Chicagoland has seen a real rise in high-wind derecho and straight-line wind events. We install cabling and bracing before storm season, not just cleanup after.

Weak-Wood Species Like Silver Maple

Common older silver maples throughout the city are prone to limb failure. We identify structural risk early with proactive pruning instead of waiting for a storm to make the decision for you.

Parkway vs. Private Tree Confusion

The city owns parkway trees; you own everything on private property. We help you understand which is which, and what to do when a hazardous city tree is stuck on a long wait list.

Root Damage to Driveways & Foundations

Mature trees planted close to older Chicago homes often push roots into driveways and foundations. We assess whether root management, not removal, can solve it.

Common Questions

What Chicago Homeowners Ask Us Most

Do I need a permit to remove a tree in Chicago?

It depends on whether the tree is on private property or a city-owned parkway. Private trees on your own property generally don't require a city permit to remove. Parkway trees are owned by the City of Chicago and are managed by the Bureau of Forestry — removal typically requires going through the city rather than a private contractor alone.

How do I know if my ash tree has emerald ash borer?

Common signs include D-shaped exit holes in the bark, thinning or dying canopy starting at the top of the tree, bark splitting with S-shaped larval tunnels underneath, and increased woodpecker activity. An ISA-certified arborist can confirm EAB and advise whether treatment or removal is the better option.

What's the difference between a parkway tree and a private tree?

A parkway tree grows in the strip of land between the sidewalk and the street and is owned and maintained by the City of Chicago's Bureau of Forestry. A private tree is anywhere on your own property and is your responsibility to maintain, insure, and remove if necessary.

Does Prime Tree Care offer 24/7 emergency tree removal?

Yes. Prime Tree Care provides around-the-clock emergency response for storm damage, fallen limbs, and trees threatening a structure or power line, across Chicago and the surrounding suburbs.
